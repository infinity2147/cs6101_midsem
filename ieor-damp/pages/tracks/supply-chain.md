## Who it suits

- You like problems with a clear business outcome: inventory, scheduling, routing, capacity.
- You want an industry role after graduating (FMCG, e-commerce, logistics, manufacturing, supply chain analytics).

## Semester by semester

| Semester | Courses | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 3 (Autumn) | IEOR core; **IE 503** Operations Analysis | Core operations models |
| 4 (Spring) | IEOR core; **IE 630** Simulation Modeling and Analysis | Simulating queues and systems is a common interview topic |
| 5 (Autumn) | **IE 501** Optimization Models (if not done), an MG or supply chain elective as IE | Modelling for planning problems |
| 6 (Spring) | **IE 616** Decision Analysis and Game Theory; one analytics elective | Decisions under uncertainty |
| 7–8 | BTP on an operations problem, ideally with industry data | Something concrete to talk about in interviews |

## Minor or honours

- A **management** minor (MG courses) helps if you are thinking of an MBA later.
- A **data science** minor helps for supply-chain analytics roles.
- Honours in IEOR is optional here; employers in this area weigh an internship more.

## Skills to build alongside

- Excel modelling (lookups, pivot tables, Solver), well enough to build a small model quickly.
- SQL and Python (pandas) for operations data.
- Inventory and queueing models you can explain without notation.

## Opportunities to watch

- **P&G Spotlight (Supply)** and other PIO programmes in 2nd year: see the [Radar]({{ '/radar/' | relative_url }}).
- The summer after 2nd year is a good time for an operations internship or a project with a prof on a real dataset.

## Honest effort note

Medium load. The courses are manageable; the extra work is the practical skills, which no course teaches you.
