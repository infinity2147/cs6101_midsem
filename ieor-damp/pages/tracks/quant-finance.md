## Who it suits

- You enjoy probability puzzles and mental maths, and you code comfortably.
- You are aiming for quant research, trading, strats or risk roles.

## Semester by semester

| Semester | Courses | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 3 (Autumn) | IEOR core; probability and statistics as early as possible | Everything in quant builds on probability |
| 4 (Spring) | IEOR core; **IE 630** Simulation Modeling and Analysis | Monte Carlo is a standard quant tool |
| 5 (Autumn) | **IE 621** Probability and Stochastic Processes I; an SI course as IE if prerequisites allow | Stochastic processes are central to finance |
| 6 (Spring) | **IE 622** Probability and Stochastic Processes II; a finance or SI elective | Depth for quant research roles |
| 7–8 | **IE 708** Markov Decision Processes; BTP with a finance or stochastic-modelling angle | Advanced material and a project to discuss |

## Minor or honours

- SI (statistics and informatics) courses are very useful but have **strict prerequisites**. Check them on ASC a semester ahead, and expect "Instructor Consent Required" on some ([FAQ]({{ '/faq/#registration' | relative_url }})).
- A finance or economics minor helps for trading or strats roles, but it matters less than your problem-solving.

## Skills to build alongside

- Probability puzzles and brainteasers (a standard quant interview book, then timed practice).
- Coding: Python and C++, plus data structures and algorithms practice for online tests.
- Statistics and basic time-series analysis.

## Opportunities to watch

- JPMorgan Chase Quant Finance Mentorship, Barclays Women in Markets, Manch by Deutsche Bank, WorldQuant IQC: see the [Radar]({{ '/radar/' | relative_url }}).

## Honest effort note

Quant hiring is very competitive and leans heavily on tests. Keep a back-up track (analytics or supply chain) going; the skills overlap.
